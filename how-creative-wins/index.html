How creative wins on Meta

Start with one person scrolling one feed. Every big idea in Meta ads is that moment, repeated billions of times. Here it is in nine steps, each with a small moving picture. Each step builds on the one before it.

Step 1 of 9

One person, one seat

Picture one person scrolling Instagram. Every few posts there’s a slot saved for an ad. Call it a seat.

As their thumb gets close to a seat, Meta has a fraction of a second to decide which ad goes there, for this person, right now. Then the next seat comes along and it decides again.

SoEverything in Meta ads comes down to winning this one seat, over and over.

Step 2 of 9

Every seat is an auction

Thousands of ads could fill that seat. Meta scores each one for this person using three things: what the advertiser will pay for a result (the bid), how likely this person is to deliver that result (Meta’s guess), and quality (people hiding or reporting an ad drags it down).

Think of it as bid times chance, plus a quality adjustment. The highest score gets the seat. Here your ad bids half what Ad A bids and still wins, because this person is two and a half times more likely to buy from it.

SoA more convincing ad beats a bigger budget, and it wins seats for less.

Step 3 of 9

Your chance is a chain

“Chance this person buys” is really a chain of small decisions, and each part of your ad owns one link. The first second stops the thumb. The middle keeps them watching. The offer earns the click. The page and the price close the sale.

Multiply the links and you get the chance Meta scores. Drag the sliders. Doubling any link doubles the result, and doubles your score in the auction.

SoThe hook loses the most people by far. That’s where creative has the most to win back.

Step 4 of 9

Your ad picks its own people

With broad targeting you don’t tell Meta who should see the ad. It shows the ad to a scattering of people, watches who responds, then looks for more people like them.

So the person on camera, the problem you name and the words you use decide your audience. Ad 1 names a newborn problem and ends up in front of new parents. Ad 2 names leg day and finds lifters. Same settings, different crowds.

SoTo reach a new group of people, make an ad for them. The creative is the targeting.

Step 5 of 9

Meta tests, then feeds what works

A new ad has no track record, so Meta spends a little on it to find out. Ads that get early results win more seats, which brings more results, which wins more seats. Spend piles onto a few ads fast.

Meta’s own guide says an ad set needs about 50 results in a week before it leaves the learning phase. Until then it’s still testing.

Check yoursIn Ads Manager, sort your ads by amount spent over the last 90 days and see how much of the money sits in the top few.

SoLosing ads are cheap. Meta stops feeding them within days. What a loser really costs you is the time it took to make.

Step 6 of 9

Nobody can call the winner in advance

Tap the ad you think will win. Then shuffle and try again. Each label is the kind of thing teams argue about in creative reviews: format, style, who’s on camera.

Our dataIn our Ad Library study of 13,336 advertisers, everything you can see in an ad at launch (format, copy, length, video or image) predicted which ads lasted only slightly better than a coin flip: 0.58 to 0.61, where 0.50 is a coin flip and 1.00 is perfect. The best predictor was the advertiser’s own track record.

SoStop trying to pick winners in a meeting. The auction picks them. Your job is to hand it good, different options.

Step 7 of 9

More real shots, more winners

If nobody can pick the winner and the hit rate stays about the same, the number of winners comes down to how many shots you take. Slide it.

Our dataAcross 8,885 Ad Library advertisers, winners grew almost one-for-one with ads launched for brands that cut losers at the same pace, and hit rate stayed flat as volume rose. About 15% of ecommerce ads in the Library become keepers, the rate used here.

Each ad still needs enough spend to be judged. Sixty ads on a budget that can only test ten leaves fifty ads nobody really saw.

SoYour hit rate mostly belongs to your brand. The number you control is how many real shots you take each week.

Step 8 of 9

Copies don’t count as shots

A new color, caption or first frame on the same video still speaks to the same person about the same problem. So it finds the same people the original found. You paid to make four ads and took one shot.

A real shot changes who the ad is for or why they’d care: a different person on camera, a different problem, a different promise, a different format.

ReportedMedia buyers widely report that since Meta’s Andromeda ad-retrieval update (late 2024), near-identical ads also get grouped and share one ticket into the auction.

SoCount shots by distinct ideas, not by files uploaded.

Step 9 of 9

The loop

Put it together and the job becomes a weekly loop. Make a batch of genuinely different ads. Launch them together. Let Meta test and feed. Then read the winners for what they share: who’s in them, which problem, which hook.

Make more of that, add some new swings, and go again. Every week adds a few winners to the pile.

SoYour edge is how many good, different shots you take each week and how well you learn from the ones that win.

The whole thing

Five lines to remember

  1. Meta sells one ad seat at a time, to the ad most likely to make that person act.
  2. Your creative sets that likelihood. The hook comes first because every later step multiplies off it.
  3. Your creative also picks the people. Who and what is in the ad decides who Meta shows it to.
  4. Most ads lose, cheaply, and nobody can tell which in advance. So winners come from the number of real shots.
  5. A shot only counts if it’s different. Read what won, make more of it, add new swings, repeat every week.

Where the numbers come from

  • Example numbers in steps 2 and 3 are made up so the arithmetic is easy to follow. Illustrations show the mechanism, not measured data.
  • Auction: Meta Business Help Center, “About ad auctions.” Meta says the winner is the ad with the highest total value, which combines the bid, estimated action rates and ad quality. Multiplying bid by chance, as in step 2, is our simplification of how the first two combine. Learning phase: Meta Business Help Center, “About the learning phase” (about 50 results in the week after the last significant edit).
  • Steps 6 and 7: our studies of the public Meta Ad Library, run in September 2026. Survival prediction covered 13,336 advertisers; volume and hit rate covered 8,885 advertisers with long histories; the ecommerce hit rate comes from the same Library data.
  • Step 8’s grouping claim comes from practitioner reports after Meta’s Andromeda update, not from a Meta document.
