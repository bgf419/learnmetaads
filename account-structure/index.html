Account structure and learning

Lesson 1 showed Meta testing your ads and feeding the ones that work. That machine learns inside containers you build: campaigns, ad sets and ads. Build them so each one gets enough results to learn from.

Step 1 of 6

Campaign, ad set, ad

Every Meta ad account has three levels. Each level holds different settings, so each kind of change happens in one place.

The campaign holds the objective: what you want people to do. If you turn on campaign budget, it holds the budget too, and Meta splits that money across the ad sets inside.

The ad set decides who, where and when: the audience, the placements (Feed, Reels, Stories and the rest), the optimisation event Meta hunts for, such as a purchase, and the schedule. The ad is what people see: the video or image, the words and the link.

Meta saysThere are six campaign objectives: Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion and Sales.

SoThe campaign says what you want. The ad set says who should see it. The ad is what they see.

Step 2 of 6

The system needs about 50 results a week

A new ad set doesn’t yet know who will act on your ads, so Meta spends part of its budget finding out. Meta calls this the learning phase.

It ends once the ad set has gathered enough results: about 50 in the week after its last significant edit. A result is the optimisation event you chose, such as a purchase.

Ad set A here gets there on day 6. Ad set B gets 25 in the week, so Meta marks it “Learning limited”. Meta says that isn’t a penalty, but B is learning from half the evidence it needs.

Meta saysAn ad set usually leaves learning “after about 50 results in the week after the ad set’s last significant edit.” It is “Learning limited” when it is unlikely to reach about 50 optimisation events in a week, which Meta calls “not a penalty”.

SoPlan every ad set to reach about 50 results a week. That number decides how many ad sets your budget can feed.

Step 3 of 6

Split the budget, split the learning

Take $3,000 a week at $40 a sale. That buys 75 sales a week. In one ad set, all 75 land in one place: well past 50.

Now drag the ad sets slider to 2. The same money buys the same 75 sales, but each ad set gets 37 or 38. Neither reaches 50, so neither leaves learning. You spent the same and the system learns less.

Raise the budget and watch how many ad sets it can feed.

Meta saysCauses of Learning limited include a small audience, low budget, a low bid or cost control, auction overlap, a rare event and “running too many ads at the same time.”

SoDivide your weekly results by 50. That’s about the most ad sets your budget can keep out of learning.

Step 4 of 6

Edits that restart learning

Learning counts from the ad set’s last significant edit. Make one and the count starts again from zero.

Meta’s list: any change to targeting, creative or optimisation event; adding an ad; pausing for 7 days or more; and changing the bid strategy. A change to the budget, bid or goal may count, depending on its size.

Some changes leave the count alone: campaign budget moving money between ad sets, adding a new ad set, or editing a different ad set. Try each edit below on an ad set that’s 32 results into its week.

Meta saysSignificant edits are any change to targeting, creative or optimization event; adding an ad; pausing for 7 days or more; and changing the bid strategy. A budget change of $100 to $1,000 may count; $100 to $101 likely won’t.

SoMake significant edits rarely, and batch them, so learning restarts once instead of five times.

Step 5 of 6

Advantage+ campaigns

Advantage+ is Meta’s name for its automated settings. A campaign counts as an Advantage+ campaign only when three of them are on: Advantage+ campaign budget, Advantage+ audience and Advantage+ placements. Turn any one off and it’s a manual campaign.

New Sales, Leads and App campaigns now start with this setup by default. Advantage+ sales campaigns replace the older Advantage+ shopping campaigns, known as ASC, and unlike ASC they can hold more than one ad set.

If you use a tool that builds campaigns through Meta’s Marketing API, it can no longer create or update ASC campaigns. That stopped with version 25.0.

Meta saysA campaign is Advantage+ only when Advantage+ campaign budget, audience and placements are all on; if any one is off, it is a manual campaign. Advantage+ sales and app campaigns are “the updated, streamlined versions” of ASC and AAC.

SoAdvantage+ is three switches, not a separate product. Turn one off only when you can name the reason.

Step 6 of 6

A simple structure that works

This course’s starting structure: one Sales campaign, a few ad sets with broad audiences, and new ads added in weekly batches. Each ad set gets enough budget to reach about 50 sales a week.

Broad works because the creative does the targeting, as lesson 1 showed. Variety comes from new ads, not new ad sets. Here $1,200 a day at $40 a sale buys 210 sales a week, enough for three ad sets of 70.

Meta’s limits are generous, so you won’t hit them with this layout. But more ads isn’t better inside one ad set: Meta advises fewer ads per ad set, with varied creative.

Meta saysEach ad set can hold up to 50 ads. A regular ad account can have 5,000 ad sets, 5,000 campaigns and 5,000 ads, of which 1,000 can be dynamic creative. Meta also advises: “Decrease ads per ad set, but maintain diverse creative assets,” because “too many ads can result in worse performance.”

ReportedMotion, a creative analytics company, recommends one campaign with campaign budget and ad sets grouped by concept or persona.

Check yoursList your active ad sets. Next to each, write its results from the last 7 days. Any under 50 is a candidate to merge.

SoFewer, fuller ad sets learn. Add variety through new ads, not new ad sets.

The whole thing

Five lines to remember

  1. The campaign holds the objective, the ad set holds the audience, placements, optimisation event and schedule, and the ad holds the creative.
  2. An ad set leaves learning after about 50 results in the week after its last significant edit.
  3. Every extra ad set splits the same results thinner, so divide your weekly results by 50 to find how many your budget can feed.
  4. Changing targeting, creative, the optimisation event or the bid strategy, adding an ad, pausing for 7 days or more, or a large budget change can restart learning.
  5. Start simple: one Sales campaign, a few full ad sets, and variety through new ads each week.
