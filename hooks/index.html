Hooks and the anatomy of an ad

Lesson 2 showed how to read the numbers as a chain. This lesson opens up the ad itself: the first 3 seconds, the parts that follow them, and how to tell from the numbers which part to fix.

Step 1 of 6

Most people leave in the first seconds

Here’s an example video ad shown to 1,000 people as they scroll. Drag the slider to see how many are still watching at each second.

The curve drops hardest at the very start. Most of the people who leave are gone before they know what the ad is about. The ones who stay past the opening tend to keep watching.

Those first 3 seconds are the hook. Everything you want to say later only reaches the people the hook kept.

ReportedBenly analysed 12,000+ ads. At the median ad, 28% of impressions became 3-second plays, so most people shown a typical video ad were gone before 3 seconds.

SoThe first 3 seconds decide who sees the rest. Spend your effort there first.

Step 2 of 6

Hook rate and hold rate

Meta reports two numbers for every video ad. 3-second video plays count plays of at least 3 seconds. ThruPlays count plays to the end, or of at least 15 seconds.

Media buyers turn those into two rates. Hook rate is 3-second plays ÷ impressions: of everyone shown the ad, how many stopped. Hold rate is ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays: of those who stopped, how many stayed.

Move the sliders. A better hook raises every number below it, because more people reach the rest of the ad.

Meta says3-second video plays and ThruPlays are Meta’s own metrics. Since March 2026, an engaged view of a video ad takes 5 seconds of watching, down from 10.

ReportedHook rate and hold rate are practitioner formulas, not Meta metrics. Benly’s 12,000+ ads put the top-quartile hook rate at 37% and the bottom quartile at 18%. Segwise calls 25% to 35% workable and above 35% strong.

SoHook rate tells you if people stopped. Hold rate tells you if they stayed. Read them separately.

Step 3 of 6

What a hook is made of

A hook isn’t one thing. In the first 3 seconds, three layers work at once:

The visual: motion, a face or a pattern break, something that doesn’t look like the post above it. The text on screen: a few big words for people watching without sound. The first spoken line: what they hear before they decide.

Switch each layer between weak and strong. A weak layer wastes the seconds you have; a strong one names a problem or shows something worth a second look.

Meta saysReels ads play with sound on unless the viewer has turned sound off.

SoWrite all three layers on purpose. Each one is a chance to stop someone.

Step 4 of 6

The body: a modular ad

After the hook, the rest of the ad has a job too. Most selling videos run in the same order: the hook, then a sales sequence (the problem, the solution, the product), then proof that it works, then an end card with the offer and a call to action (CTA), the line that tells people what to do next.

Because the parts are separate, you can swap them. Take the hook from one ad, the sales sequence from a second and the end card from a third. The course calls this a Frankenstein brief.

Press a swap button. One part changes; the rest of the ad stays the same.

SoAn ad is four parts, not one. Build each part so it can be swapped.

Step 5 of 6

Format, placements and safe zones

Your ad shows in different places, called placements, and each has its own shape. Reels and Stories fill a tall 9:16 screen. Feed shows a shorter frame.

On Reels, the app lays its own buttons, the account name and the caption over your video. Keep your words and faces in the middle, the safe zone, where nothing covers them. Tap the frame, or the buttons, to switch between Reels and a 4:5 Feed cut.

Meta saysUse 9:16 for Stories and Reels. Facebook Feed video is 4:5. Meta’s Instagram Feed guidance mentions both 9:16 and 1:1 or 4:5, so check the options in Ads Manager.

Meta saysIn a meta-analysis of 15 Reels split tests, 9:16 video with sound and messages inside the safe zone had 34.5% lower cost per result than image ads. Meta doesn’t say when the tests ran.

SoShoot tall, keep the message in the middle, and the same video works in Reels and Feed.

Step 6 of 6

Swap one part at a time

Each number points at one part of the ad. Read them in order, like lesson 2’s chain, and stop at the first one that’s low for your account.

Few people stop: change the hook. They stop, then leave: change the body. They watch but don’t click: change the offer or the CTA. They click but don’t buy: the ad did its job, so fix the page. (Conversion rate is purchases ÷ link clicks, as in lesson 2.)

“Low” means low for you. Compare each ad with your account’s usual, not with someone else’s benchmark.

Meta saysWhen you test, test one variable at a time.

Check yoursIn Ads Manager, add the columns Impressions, 3-second video plays, ThruPlays and Link clicks next to your purchases. Work out the four rates for your last five videos and find the first one that’s low.

SoChange the part the numbers point to, and nothing else. Then you know what the change was worth.

The whole thing

Five lines to remember

  1. Most people decide in the first seconds, so the hook decides who sees the rest.
  2. Hook rate is 3-second plays ÷ impressions; hold rate is ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays.
  3. A hook has three layers: the visual, the text on screen and the first spoken line.
  4. After the hook come a sales sequence, proof and an end card, and each part can be swapped.
  5. Find the first low number, change the one part it points to, and keep the rest.

Where the numbers come from

  • Example numbers are made up to show the method: the 1,000-viewer curve, the 10,000 impressions, the ads A, B and C, and every rate in step 6. The hook layers, the stop-power meter and the screen zones are illustrations, not measurements.
  • Video metrics: Meta’s definitions of 3-second video plays and ThruPlays. The 5-second engaged view: Meta, March 2026.
  • Hook rate and hold rate formulas and the 25% to 35% band: Segwise. The 12,000+ ad medians and quartiles: Benly, 2026. Both are practitioner sources, not Meta.
  • Sound on, safe zones and the 34.5% meta-analysis of 15 Reels split tests: Meta, Reels ads. Aspect ratios: Meta, 9:16 placements, Facebook Feed video and Instagram Feed video.
  • Test one variable at a time: Meta, A/B testing.
  • The three hook layers, the modular ad, the Frankenstein brief and the symptom-to-part map are the course’s method, not measured results.
