The numbers behind a sale

Lesson 1 followed one seat. Now count them. Every sale you buy on Meta comes out of a short chain: what views cost, how many viewers click, and how many clickers buy. Learn the chain once and you can read any ad account.

Step 1 of 6

You pay for views

Meta doesn’t charge you for sales. It charges you each time your ad fills a seat. Each of those is an impression.

The price is quoted per thousand impressions, called CPM (cost per mille). Here 1,000 views cost $12, so the CPM is $12 and each view costs 1.2 cents.

CPM is set by the auction. It rises when more advertisers chase the same people, as in the weeks before the holidays. It falls when your ad scores well for the people it reaches.

Meta saysCPM is the average cost of 1,000 impressions.

SoYou buy views. Turning them into sales is the job of everything after the view.

Step 2 of 6

Some views become clicks

Most people scroll past. A few tap through to your site. The share who do is the link click-through rate, or CTR.

Here 12 of the 1,000 viewers click, so the link CTR is 1.2%. The $12 those views cost is now spread over 12 clicks: $1 a click.

Ads Manager also shows a column called CTR (all). This course uses link CTR: only the clicks that land on your site.

Meta saysLink CTR is link clicks divided by impressions.

SoCTR is the ad’s report card for stopping the thumb and earning the tap.

Step 3 of 6

Some clicks become sales

Of the people who reach your site, some buy. The share who do is the conversion rate. At 2.5%, 12 clicks make 0.3 of a sale, which is hard to picture, so zoom out to 10,000 views.

$120 buys 10,000 views. 120 people click. 3 of them buy. Each sale cost $120 ÷ 3 = $40. That’s the cost per purchase, often called CPA.

If each order is $60, $120 of ads brought in $180 of sales. Sales divided by ad spend is ROAS, return on ad spend: $180 ÷ $120 = 1.5.

SoConversion rate belongs mostly to the page, the price and whether the page delivers what the ad promised.

Step 4 of 6

One formula

Steps 1 to 3 fit in one line:

Cost per sale = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR × conversion rate)

Drag the sliders. Doubling CTR cuts the cost of a sale in half, exactly as much as halving CPM does. Each link pulls the same weight.

Creative moves all three. A better hook and offer lift CTR. An ad whose promise matches the page lifts conversion rate. And as lesson 1 showed, an ad people respond to wins its seats for less, which lowers CPM.

SoWhen a sale costs more than it’s worth, any of the three links can fix it. Find the weakest one first.

Step 5 of 6

Find the broken link

Here are three ads from one account. All three cost about twice the account’s usual $40 a sale. They look like the same problem. They aren’t.

Tap each ad. Ad A’s viewers don’t click: the hook or the offer isn’t landing. Ad B gets plenty of clicks that don’t buy: the ad promises something the page doesn’t deliver. Ad C converts fine but pays double for its seats: people skip or hide it, or it’s chasing an expensive crowd.

For video ads, the click link splits in two: did people stop, and did they keep watching? Lesson 5 covers both.

Check yoursSort your ads by cost per purchase. Take the three worst that have real spend and write down which link broke for each.

SoThe same bad cost per sale has three different causes and three different fixes. Read the chain before you change anything.

Step 6 of 6

Run your own numbers

Every number in this lesson is a column in Ads Manager. Type in one ad’s numbers, or a whole campaign’s, and the chain fills in.

One catch. Meta’s purchase count includes some sales from people who saw the ad and never clicked, so the conversion rate you work out here runs a little high. Lesson 10 explains how Meta counts sales.

Check yoursIn Ads Manager, add the columns Amount spent, Impressions, Link clicks, Purchases and Purchases conversion value, then copy one ad’s row into the boxes.

SoFive numbers from Ads Manager give you the whole chain. Work it out for your best and worst ads and compare.

The whole thing

Five lines to remember

  1. You pay for views. CPM is the price of 1,000 of them, and the auction sets it.
  2. Views become clicks at the link CTR. Clicks become sales at the conversion rate.
  3. Cost per sale = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR × conversion rate). ROAS = order value ÷ cost per sale.
  4. Every link pulls equal weight. Double any rate, or halve CPM, and a sale costs half as much.
  5. When sales cost too much, find the broken link first. Low CTR is the ad, low conversion is the page or the promise, high CPM is the seats.

Where the numbers come from

  • Example numbers in every step are made up so the arithmetic is easy to follow.
  • CPM and cost per result: Meta, “Optimize your ad campaign”. CPM is the average cost of 1,000 impressions; cost per result is the average cost of whatever result the campaign optimises for.
  • Link CTR: Meta Business Help Center, link clicks divided by impressions.
  • Step 6’s view-through point: in Meta’s Insights API, the default attribution setting is 7-day click plus 1-day view (Ad insights reference, v25.0). Lesson 10 covers attribution in full.
