The weekly playbook

Ten lessons, one routine. Every week you read the numbers, read the creative, make the next batch, launch it and check the account’s health. Each step links back to the lesson that explains it, starting with why creative wins.

Step 1 of 5

Monday: read the numbers

Start the week with the account, not the ads. Ask three questions, in this order.

Is cost per purchase inside your target? Your target and breakeven come from lesson 3. Here it’s $24.00: over the $21.60 target, still under the $27.00 breakeven.

Which link broke? Cost per sale = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR × conversion rate), from lesson 2. CPM and conversion rate held. CTR fell from 1.5% to 1.25%, which lifts cost per sale from $20 to $24. Low CTR is the ad, not the page.

Is each ad set learning? An ad set that’s still learning hasn’t settled yet, so judge it gently (lesson 7).

Meta saysAn ad set leaves learning usually after about 50 results in the week after its last significant edit. When it’s unlikely to reach about 50 optimisation events in a week, Meta labels it Learning limited, and says that is “not a penalty”.

Check yoursPut this week’s cost per purchase next to your target and breakeven from lesson 3. Then put this week’s CPM, CTR and conversion rate next to last month’s, and circle the one that moved.

SoRead the account before the ads: target first, then the broken link, then learning. The broken link tells you what to fix this week.

Step 2 of 5

Read the creative

Now open the ads. For each one, ask what it’s telling you and what to make next. Tap an ad, or use the buttons.

Read a video ad in the order a viewer meets it: the hook (did they stop?), the hold (did they keep watching?), the click (did they tap?), then the sale. The first weak link tells you which part to change (lesson 5). Compare each ad with your own account’s usual numbers, not someone else’s benchmark.

A winner gets variants that each change one part (lesson 6). An ad with almost no spend is untested, not lost (lesson 9).

Meta saysA 3-second video play counts plays of at least 3 seconds, or nearly the whole video if it’s shorter. A ThruPlay counts plays to completion or for at least 15 seconds.

ReportedPractitioners define hook rate as 3-second plays ÷ impressions and hold rate as ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays. Neither is a Meta metric.

SoEvery ad’s numbers point at one part to change. Fix that part and keep the rest.

Step 3 of 5

Brief and make

Decide how many new winners you need a month, then work backwards. At a 15% keeper rate, each winner takes about 7 ads.

Our dataAcross 8,885 Ad Library advertisers, winners grew almost one-for-one with ads launched for brands that cut losers at the same pace, and hit rate stayed flat as volume rose. About 15% of ecommerce ads in the Library become keepers, the rate used here.

Split each week’s batch. Most of it exploits: variants of this month’s winners. The rest explores: new concepts with a different audience, angle, offer or ad type (lesson 4). The course’s starting split is 60–70% exploit and 30–40% explore (lesson 6).

The stars mark the 15% rate, not which ads will win. Nobody can tell that in advance (lesson 1).

Meta saysBuild a variety of creative so Meta’s AI can match the message to the person.

Check yoursCount how many ads you launched in each of the last four weeks, and how many became keepers. That’s your own keeper rate.

SoSet the weekly number from the winners you need, and keep a share of every batch for new concepts.

Step 4 of 5

Launch in batches

Adding an ad to an ad set is a significant edit, and a significant edit restarts learning. So launch once a week: put the whole batch into an ad set that already works, in one go (lesson 7).

Compare the two plans. Same ad set, same 10 results a day. Added in one batch on Monday, the ad set reaches about 50 results by Friday. Added one ad a day, the count restarts every morning and never gets there.

In the same sitting, apply your kill and keep rules from lesson 9. Turn an ad off once it has spent twice your target cost per purchase without a sale. Keep an ad whose cost per purchase stays at or under target after enough sales. These are the course’s rules of thumb. Raise budgets in steps, not jumps (lesson 8).

Meta saysSignificant edits include any change to targeting, creative or optimisation event, adding an ad, pausing for 7 days or more, and changing the bid strategy. A budget change may count too, depending on its size.

Meta saysEach ad set can hold up to 50 ads. Meta says “too many ads can result in worse performance” and advises fewer ads per ad set, with diverse creative.

SoOne batch, once a week, into an ad set that already works. Fewer resets means more weeks out of learning.

Step 5 of 5

The account health checklist

Last, check the account itself. Tick what’s true this week. Each line links to the lesson that explains it.

If your browser allows it, your ticks stay on this device so you can come back next week. Press Clear to start a fresh week.

Anything you can’t tick becomes a task for next Monday’s read, and the loop starts again.

SoRun the loop every week: numbers, creative, make, launch, health. Each pass gives the next one better numbers to read.

The whole thing

Five lines to remember

  1. Every week runs the same loop: read the numbers, read the creative, make, launch, and check the account’s health.
  2. Read the account first: cost per purchase against your target, then the broken link, then learning.
  3. Read each ad in the order a viewer meets it, and change only the part that broke.
  4. Set the weekly batch from the winners you need, at about 7 ads per winner, with a share kept for new concepts.
  5. Launch the batch in one go into an ad set that already works, so learning restarts once a week, not every day.
